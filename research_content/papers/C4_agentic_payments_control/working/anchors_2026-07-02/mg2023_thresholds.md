Section 2.1 heading (verbatim):

2.1. Guideline 1: Mergers Raise a Presumption of Illegality When They Significantly Increase Concentration in a Highly Concentrated Market.

Concentration threshold passage (verbatim; footnote callouts 12-16 appear in source):

The Agencies generally measure concentration levels using the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index ("HHI"). The HHI is defined as the sum of the squares of the market shares; it is small when there are many small firms and grows larger as the market becomes more concentrated, reaching 10,000 in a market with a single firm. Markets with an HHI greater than 1,800 are highly concentrated, and a change of more than 100 points is a significant increase. A merger that creates or further consolidates a highly concentrated market that involves an increase in the HHI of more than 100 points is presumed to substantially lessen competition or tend to create a monopoly. The Agencies also may examine the market share of the merged firm: a merger that creates a firm with a share over thirty percent is also presumed to substantially lessen competition or tend to create a monopoly if it also involves an increase in HHI of more than 100 points.

This presumption of illegality can be rebutted or disproved. The higher the concentration metrics over these thresholds, the greater the risk to competition suggested by this market structure analysis and the stronger the evidence needed to rebut or disprove it.

Verifier note (authored, not source text): the current (December 18, 2023) Merger Guidelines set the highly-concentrated threshold at HHI greater than 1,800 (not 2,500) in Guideline 1 (document section 2.1), with a structural presumption of illegality at post-merger HHI greater than 1,800 plus change in HHI greater than 100, or merged-firm share greater than 30% plus change in HHI greater than 100. This is LOWER than the 2010 guidelines' 2,500 threshold, and the 2010 guidelines were withdrawn in December 2023 per the DOJ archive banner. The DOJ 2023 Merger Guidelines page also links an Acting Assistant Attorney General's memorandum on "Use of 2023 Merger Guidelines" (https://www.justice.gov/atr/media/1389861/dl?inline), consistent with the 2023 guidelines remaining the operative document.
